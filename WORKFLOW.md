# Workflow

## 1. Assign bounded work

The Seer defines the objective and the mandate. A work order specifies scope, prohibited actions, acceptance criteria, evidence expectations, and escalation conditions. Relevant worker contracts and governance policies must also be considered; the illustrative work order is not a substitute for them.

## 2. Produce a proposal

The Maker prepares a diff and supporting evidence. It distinguishes what it observed from what it merely asserts. In this example, the Maker may propose a documentation change but may not merge or deploy it.

## 3. Check the proposal

The Checker evaluates each criterion and records findings and conditions. Using a separate agent does not establish independence by itself: model/provider, shared context, tools, and evidence sources must be assessed. Human review remains available for uncertainty or insufficient independence.

## 4. Resolve findings

The Maker addresses conditions and submits revised evidence. Material revisions require renewed checking. Work exceeding its mandate is held or escalated rather than silently expanded.

## 5. Record acceptance

The human Buyer records acceptance, conditional acceptance, or rejection, with reasons. The example uses ACCEPT only because the linked illustrative Checker record reports a pass with no unresolved findings.

## 6. Authorize release separately

A release requires its own authorization and verified implementation checks. This example ends at acceptance and contains no deployment record. Acceptance is not evidence that a change was merged, deployed, or achieved its intended business outcome.

## 7. Review the outcome

In a real cycle, verify the released state, evaluate the objective, and preserve the evidence needed for closure. Any reported benefit must distinguish measured outcomes from expected benefits.
